# Steps to create the channel — A Quiet Little Place

## Phase 1: Account and name (day 1)
1. Create (or use) a Google account for the channel. Use a dedicated email, not your personal one.
2. Open YouTube and sign in. Click your profile → **Create a channel**. Choose **Use a custom name** to create a **Brand Account** (allows adding managers later).
3. Name: **A Quiet Little Place** (if taken, check variations: "A Quiet Little Place Kids", "Quiet Little Place Music").
4. Handle: `@aquietlittleplace` (YouTube Studio → Customization → Basic info). No spaces. Check availability.
5. Search YouTube and Google for the name first: confirm there's no similar kids' channel or trademark conflict.

## Phase 2: Branding (days 1–2)
6. Profile picture: 800×800 px, watercolor moon/lake from the palette.
7. Banner: 2560×1440 px (keep important content in the central 1546×423 safe area). Include the tagline.
8. Description: paste the long description from `descripciones-en.md`.
9. Keywords: add in Studio → Settings → Channel → Basic info.
10. Country: United States. Language: English.
11. Channel contact email (business inquiries): use the dedicated email (it is visible to the public, so don't use a personal one).

## Phase 3: Required settings (day 2)
12. Studio → Settings → Channel → **Audience**: set **"Yes, set this channel as made for kids"** (applies to all videos). This is mandatory for this content.
13. Understand what that does: comments are off, no personalized ads, no notifications bell, some features are limited. Plan a separate contact method for teachers (a simple form or email in the About section).
14. Enable **two-step verification** on the Google account.
15. Add trusted collaborators via Brand Account permissions if needed (optional).

## Phase 4: Production before publishing (week 1)
16. Produce the **pilot video** (3–5 min): the lake scene + short story (Story 1, shortened) to test music, animation and narration.
17. Use only original or properly licensed music. Keep a folder per video with license proof, prompts or project files, and the script.
18. Write the script and illustrations yourself or with AI as a tool, but add real creative value: original story, original music, your own edits. Fully automated mass-produced videos risk demonetization ("inauthentic content").
19. Build a **video template**: same intro (3–5 seconds, soft), same progress bar (Music → Draw → Story), same ending card.
20. Add captions in English (accessibility and teachers) and a clear thumbnail in the palette (no clickbait, no bright colors).

## Phase 5: Publishing (weeks 1–4)
21. Upload the pilot privately and check on a phone, TV and tablet. Check audio levels (no sudden loud sounds, keep consistent volume).
22. Upload with the right fields: title, timeline chapters (first at 0:00), description, tags, thumbnail, **Made for Kids** selected, playlist.
23. Create playlists: Full Routine, Story Time, Draw Along, Transition Timers, Calm Down Corner, Nap Time.
24. Publish on a fixed schedule (for example one video per week, same day and time). Consistency helps both algorithm and teachers.
25. Weeks 2–4: publish videos 2–6 as planned (two instrumental, one story, one rest-time).

## Phase 6: Reach schools and daycares (weeks 2–6)
26. Make the **free printable guide + coloring page** for each video. Host on a simple page or in the video description link.
27. Share the channel with local daycares, preschools and learning centers (a short, friendly message with the link and the guide).
28. Add a clear line in each description: "Free to play in classrooms, daycares and centers. Please do not re-upload."

## Phase 7: Monetization (month 3+)
29. YouTube Partner Program: requirements change, so check the current ones in Studio → Earn. Typically 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months (or 10M Shorts views), with an entry tier at lower thresholds for some features.
30. Link AdSense only when YouTube invites you to apply.
31. Content for kids earns less per view (limited ads). Plan other income: ad-free licensed packs for schools, digital albums, printable guides, Spotify.

## Checklist before pressing "Publish"
- [ ] Made for Kids selected
- [ ] No loud sounds or sudden changes
- [ ] Music licensed and proof saved
- [ ] No third-party characters or logos
- [ ] Timeline chapters correct
- [ ] Thumbnail in palette, readable on a phone
- [ ] Description includes guide link and usage note
- [ ] Not near-identical to the previous video

## Legal and safety notes
- Children on camera: avoid. If used, written parental consent, hands only, no names, no faces.
- Privacy: don't collect children's data. Don't ask kids to contact you.
- Not therapy or medical: use words like "calm", "gentle", "predictable".
- Have the usage terms for schools reviewed by a professional if you plan to offer licenses.
