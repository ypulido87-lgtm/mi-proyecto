# Sample stories 2, 3 and 4

Same format as Story 1: one calm narrator, very soft piano underneath (lower during dialogue), pause 2–3 seconds at "…". Each is about 5–6 minutes.

---

# Story 2 — The Bunny Who Counted Stars

**Value:** sharing
**Video:** 2, "Stars Over the Forest"

**[Scene: a hill at dusk, soft purple-blue sky, first stars appearing]**

On a quiet hill at the edge of the forest lived a little bunny named Momo.

Every night, when the sky turned dark and soft, Momo climbed to the very top of the hill.

There was a flat, smooth rock there. It was the best place in the whole forest to see the stars.

…

Momo lay down on the rock and began to count.

"One star… two stars… three stars…"

The stars twinkled slowly, one by one.

"Four… five… six…"

…

**[Scene: a small hedgehog climbing the hill, looking up]**

Just then, Momo heard a soft rustle.

A little hedgehog was climbing the hill. His name was Bram.

"Hello, Momo," said Bram quietly. "I wanted to see the stars too. But the trees are in my way down there."

Momo looked at the rock. It was just big enough for one little bunny.

Momo felt a little worried.

"This is my special place," Momo thought.

…

But then Momo looked at Bram. Bram was looking up at the sky with big, hopeful eyes.

Momo took a slow breath.

"Would you like to sit with me?" said Momo. "There is room if we sit close."

Bram's face lit up.

"Really? Thank you, Momo!"

…

**[Scene: bunny and hedgehog side by side on the rock, a few more stars]**

Momo moved over. Bram climbed up. They sat side by side on the smooth rock.

"One star," whispered Bram.

"Two stars," whispered Momo.

They counted together.

…

Soon, a sleepy owl landed on a nearby branch.

"What are you counting?" asked the owl.

"Stars," said Momo. "Would you like to count with us?"

"I would," said the owl. "Thank you for sharing."

…

**[Scene: the rock, now with a bunny, a hedgehog, an owl nearby, full starry sky]**

Then a little deer came. Then a tiny mouse.

Each time, Momo and Bram made a little more room.

And the rock, which had felt so small, somehow felt big enough for everyone.

…

The sky was full of stars now. Too many to count.

"I lost count," laughed Momo softly.

"Me too," said Bram.

"That's okay," said the owl. "Stars are nicer when we watch them together."

…

Momo felt warm and happy.

When Momo had the rock all alone, the stars were beautiful.

But when Momo shared the rock, the stars were beautiful, and the night felt full of friends.

…

*The end.*

**Think and talk**
1. What did Momo do when Bram came?
2. How did Momo feel when sharing?
3. What can you share with a friend today?

# Story 3 — The Leaf That Floated Home

**Value:** helping others
**Video:** 3, "The Little River"

**[Scene: a small waterfall and stream, soft green light, leaves on the water]**

Far up the hill, a little river began to flow.

It ran over smooth stones and under tall green trees.

One autumn morning, a single golden leaf fell from a branch and landed on the water.

"Oh," said the leaf. "Where am I going?"

"Down the river," said the water gently. "Down, and down."

…

The leaf floated along. Past the mossy stones. Past the ferns.

But soon, the leaf came to a place where the river made a little bend.

A small twig was stuck there. The leaf was caught.

"Oh no," said the leaf. "I cannot move."

…

**[Scene: a frog on a lily pad, looking down]**

A little green frog was sitting on a nearby stone.

"You look stuck," said the frog.

"I am," said the leaf softly. "I want to go home, but I cannot move."

The frog thought for a moment.

"I can help," said the frog.

…

The frog hopped down. He gave the twig a gentle push.

Pop. The twig came loose.

"Thank you, frog!" said the leaf.

"You're welcome," said the frog. "Helping is a kind thing to do."

…

**[Scene: the river widens; a family of otters playing]**

The leaf floated on.

Soon the river got wider and faster.

Two otters were playing in the water. "Where are you going, little leaf?" asked the first otter.

"Home," said the leaf. "To the big tree at the end of the river."

"We know that tree!" said the second otter. "It's far. Let us help you."

The otters swam on either side of the leaf. They made a gentle ripple that carried the leaf along.

…

**[Scene: a calm pond, a big old oak tree on the shore]**

At last, the river slowed down. It opened into a calm pond.

And there, on the shore, stood the big old oak tree.

"Home!" said the leaf.

…

But the leaf was too far from the shore to reach it.

A little duck heard the leaf's soft voice.

"I can help," said the duck.

The duck nudged the leaf, slowly, softly, until it touched the grass at the foot of the tree.

…

The leaf rested on the soft ground.

"Thank you, frog," said the leaf. "Thank you, otters. Thank you, duck."

"You're welcome," said all the animals.

…

The sun set slowly. The pond turned golden.

And the little leaf, safe at home, learned something very special:

When we ask for help, kind friends come.

And when we help, our hearts feel light, like a leaf floating on the water.

…

*The end.*

**Think and talk**
1. Who helped the leaf?
2. How did they help?
3. How can you help someone at school today?

# Story 4 — The Butterfly and the Little Bear

**Value:** respect
**Video:** 4, "A Walk Among Flowers"

**[Scene: a sunny meadow, tall flowers, soft light]**

In a green meadow, full of flowers, lived a little bear named Tobi.

Tobi was big and soft and curious. He loved to explore.

One sunny afternoon, Tobi walked slowly through the flowers, sniffing and smiling.

…

Then he saw something beautiful.

A butterfly, with wings the color of the sky, was resting on a daisy.

"Oh!" whispered Tobi. "So pretty!"

…

Tobi wanted to touch the butterfly. His paw reached out, slowly.

But then he stopped.

"Wait," Tobi thought. "She is resting. If I touch her, she might be scared."

…

**[Scene: the bear sitting close, hands in his lap, looking at the butterfly]**

Tobi pulled his paw back.

He sat down on the grass, very quietly.

He watched the butterfly from a gentle distance.

"Hello, butterfly," he whispered. "I will not touch you. I will just watch."

The butterfly's wings opened and closed, slowly.

…

"Thank you," said the butterfly softly. "Not everyone is so gentle."

"I want to be kind," said Tobi. "You were here first. This is your flower."

The butterfly smiled.

…

**[Scene: a bee arrives; the bear moves back to make space]**

A busy bee arrived at the next flower.

"Bzzz," said the bee. "May I have some nectar?"

"Of course," said Tobi, stepping back. "I will give you space."

…

A small rabbit came hopping. A bird landed on a branch.

Each time, Tobi said hello quietly. Each time, he gave them room.

"You are very respectful, Tobi," said the rabbit.

"What does that mean?" asked Tobi.

"It means," said the rabbit, "you treat everyone gently, with kind words and kind hands."

…

**[Scene: the butterfly flutters up and rests on the bear's nose, warm sunset]**

The sun began to set. The meadow turned golden.

The butterfly lifted off her flower. She fluttered up, up, and then she landed, very lightly, on Tobi's nose.

Tobi stayed completely still. He barely breathed.

"Thank you for being gentle," whispered the butterfly.

"Thank you for trusting me," whispered Tobi.

…

Tobi learned something very special that day:

When we are gentle, others feel safe.

When we use kind words and kind hands, friendship comes.

And when we respect others, they respect us back.

…

*The end.*

**Think and talk**
1. What did Tobi do instead of touching the butterfly?
2. How did the butterfly feel?
3. What are kind words and kind hands?
