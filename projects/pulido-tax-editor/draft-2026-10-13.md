## English version
**Form 1099-NEC Threshold Rises from $600 to $2,000: What to Know Before Paying Contractors in 2026**

If you hire freelancers or contractors, one change is worth knowing before the year ends: starting in 2026, the threshold for Form 1099-NEC went from $600 to **$2,000**.

**What changed.** Form 1099-NEC reports to the IRS what a business paid to non-employees (independent contractors) for services. It used to be required at $600 or more paid to one person in a year. Per the IRS instructions for these forms, nonemployee compensation of $2,000 or more is now reported, applying to tax years beginning after 2025 — that is, 2026 payments reported in 2027. The IRS also notes the $2,000 figure may be adjusted for inflation beginning in 2027.

**What does not change.** Income is still income: a payer not filing a 1099 does not make the contractor's earnings non-taxable. 2025 payments follow the old rule. If you applied backup withholding, you must file the 1099-NEC regardless of the amount. This is the 1099-NEC rule; payments on Form 1099-MISC have their own thresholds.

**Dates.** The 1099-NEC is due to the contractor and the IRS by January 31. In 2027 that is a Sunday; per the IRS, due dates falling on a weekend or holiday move to the next business day (Monday, February 1, 2027).

**What to do now.** List everyone you paid in 2026 and total each; request a Form W-9 from new contractors before the first payment; keep contractor payments documented with invoices and dates; keep receipts even when no 1099 is required; if you operate outside Florida, check that state's own reporting rules.

**Key takeaway.** Less paperwork for small payments, but no less need for good records. October is a good time to review what you've paid each contractor.
